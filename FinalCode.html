Built for Bloodshed: Why U.S. Mass Shootings Won’t Stop Anytime Soon

By Spencer Jacobs | April 2026

Picture by Harvard Gazette

April 20th, 1999, December 14th, 2012, and February 14th, 2018. These dates reflect the Columbine, Sandy Hook, and Parkland shootings, serving as unforgettable reminders of the sinister capabilities man possesses. But beyond that, they represent our nation’s failure to learn from past mistakes and invite repetition. While mass shootings can be an uncomfortable subject, it is necessary to discuss them. Ignorance of the political and social factors that enable such atrocities to occur creates a cycle of never-ending suffering. Thoughts and prayers are not nearly enough.

“Mass Shooting” is a rather loose term which may not give the public a clear image of the crime. The Gun Violence Archive (GVA) defines a "mass shooting" as an incident where there is "a minimum of four victims shot, not including any shooter." And while this concept is often associated with schools, the large majority of mass shootings occur in other settings. The most common locations are actually parties, weddings and funerals, and clubs. Drive-by shootings are also quite common. Mass shootings are ubiquitous when there is constant access to weapons of destruction; nowhere is truly safe.

The Current State Of America:

Grave concern for mass shootings should not come as a surprise to anyone paying attention, as the timeline leading up to the present day is filled with signs. The number of mass shooting total victims and fatalities has exponentially increased over the past 23 years in spite of a stagnant number of wounded. This paradox leads to the concerning truth that mass shootings now have a high mortality rate. Today, Americans are 26 times more likely to be shot and killed than others in high-income countries. Every day, 327 people, including 23 minors, are shot in the United States. Of those, on average, 117 will die.

Excluding 2020, when everyone was quarantined, from 2014 to its 2021 peak, mass shootings increased by 1,811%. When a percentage reaches that level and becomes so inconsiderably high, it loses meaning. Imagine you are driving a car at 100 mph. This is an incredibly fast speed which can incur serious risk in the wrong situation. An 1811% increase to this 100 mph speed adds 1,811 mph to your current pace and propels you like a rocket, shattering the sound barrier at Mach 2.5. The 19 mass shootings in 2014 feel like a lot, but when put up against the 363 in 2021, the epidemic reaches “rocket-level” speed.

Recent American history supports the notion that semi-automatic weapons are responsible for many of the massacres that take place. Following the 1994 assault weapon ban, gun massacres dropped by a staggering 37% during the decade. The 15 million assault weapons in the public’s possession are difficult to remove through policy, but by outlawing the usage of such weapons, Congress hoped to discourage usage and mitigate their involvement in human casualties. It is then no surprise that following the ban's removal, the frequency of mass shootings skyrocketed and well surpassed prior levels. This historical milestone unfortunately has not promoted much legislative change despite valiant efforts. Access to semi-automatic weapons creates an abundance of additional shootings on the spot.

Misleading Baseline Stats:

Surface-level examination of mass shootings in the U.S. since 2014 suggests a lack of political correlation. Texas, California, Illinois, Pennsylvania, and Florida comprise the top 5 states with the most shootings and clearly vary on the ideological spectrum. However, this is more a byproduct of how state populations reside than political contribution. The rankings serve as a population metric since these states are all among the most populated.

A level deeper, urban areas have the most mass shootings within these states. But similar to the states, this is a population statistic and not reflective of the true story. In fact, small towns house a disproportionate amount of the biggest school shootings: of the 10 deadliest school shootings in the U.S., all but one happened in towns with fewer than 75,000 residents. Licensed clinical counselor Sunny Mueller offered her perspective on this relationship:

"In rural communities, mental health care is severely lacking. Erasing the stigma around mental illness, or any kind of mental struggle whatsoever, and making sure that mental help is available to kids — in schools, outside of schools."

The radicalization of shooters has greatly contributed to this troublesome proportion. There has arguably never been a more isolating time in modern history. The barrier to access social media is at an all-time low, and the algorithms developed by platform creators have put a stronghold on their users. Per the Digital Marketing Institute, the purpose of these algorithms is “to filter out irrelevant content or content that’s not high quality to…ensure your content is high quality, relevant and appealing." Time consumption and heavy usage are the prizes in mind when creating these algorithms. With how easy it is to build an echo-chamber through social media, one’s dark thoughts can become heavily reinforced. These platforms also often have communities built into them that promote their perspectives above all.

If a lost soul is unfortunate enough to stumble across a group of people angry at the world and ready to act, they can find themselves unintentionally becoming mobilized. These communities and algorithms combine to anger the user; if that anger builds and there is not a healthy way to channel it, they can unleash their rage on the innocent. This, however, only represents a portion of the mass shootings, since many of these shootings do not include such premeditated thought. Mueller’s thoughts are representative of a broader idea that isolation left untreated can prompt kids to do terrible things. And despite the distant look that no political party in particular is to blame, a clear instigator emerges.

The Impact Of The NRA:

The average number of mass shootings in "Blue" states (39.25) is virtually identical to that of “Red" states (39.17). This negligible difference masks the stark contrasts in ideology surrounding both parties. There is one organization above all others that enables continued ownership of such weapons. Founded in 1871 by Union veterans Col. William C. Church and Gen. George Wingate, the National Rifle Association (NRA) was originally established to "promote and encourage rifle shooting on a scientific basis." This proclamation came following a lack of shooting ability observed during the Civil War. The NRA’s founding ideals centered on training, youth programs, and the development of world-class shooting facilities like Camp Perry, but rapidly expanded over the following century. In response to increasing legislative challenges, the association formed its Legislative Affairs Division in 1934 and the Institute for Legislative Action (ILA) in 1975 to defend Second Amendment privileges. During the 1970s, major lobbying initiatives blossomed to aid the quest for securing gun rights. The NRA now “serves” to satisfy the interests of gun fanatics, second amendment enthusiasts, and hunters across the country. Though the nature of their work is more about retaining power than ensuring safety for the average American. They argue that the interpretation of the constitution obliges the government to not infringe upon an American’s right to “bear arms.” Today, the NRA is a political juggernaut, aiding Republicans’ pursuit to hold office and even the presidency.

The 2016 election cycle marked a milestone for the NRA’s political progression, emerging as a leading conservative force. In total, they spent over $50 million to support Donald Trump and other Republican nominees. The presidential race in particular assumed most of the allocated funds. Logically speaking, it can be deeply unpopular and dangerous for a congressperson to go against the president, especially if they belong to the same party. Voters often lack the political knowledge to make informed choices at the district level and look to recommendations from the more polarizing leaders. As a result, if President Trump openly advocated to not reelect a certain republican congressperson, the president will have major influence on the outcome of that election. Therefore, when in office, you must “watch your mouth.” The NRA cleverly funded both pro-Trump and Anti-Clinton campaign ads. Negative campaign ads lead into a negative frame that can provoke disapproving thought in people that becomes hard to deviate from.

Political alignments play such a key role in the current state of mass shootings. Politicians want to stay in office above all, and donor security greatly aids that objective. If you were promised another term simply by refusing to support a bill, there is little downside to taking that deal. Republicans are the first ones to offer their thoughts and prayers when these atrocities persist, but receive funding from the very people supplying the weapons to their constituents.

Where Shootings Really Happen:

School shootings, while by far the most salient, make up just a fraction of the carnage. Media outlets know that to maximize attention, showing off tragedy is effective. Rather than problem solve, the most polarizing networks, such as CNN and FOX News will attack or defend the credentials of the shooter to ensure political safety. Even people who lead independent lives find themselves sucked into the media's antics by feeling the need to denounce the politicization of children getting shot. Understanding the “why” becomes lost in translation when billion-dollar corporations implant their views directly into our thoughts without consent. Rather than keep the people informed, they keep us angry.

There is nowhere in America that is safe from somebody’s inner demons getting the best of them and taking it out on the world. Parties, social gatherings, bars, and nightlife shootings are common occurrences. These categories are synonymous with drug and alcohol usage, which are notorious enablers of destructive tendency. A loner in a city, feeling the effects of isolation and with alcohol in their system, could become far more inclined to act on dark instincts. The voice of reason dissipates at gatherings given the social pressures to fit in and conform to the opinions of others. On the flip side, anger from being pressed to conform can also lead to an escalated situation where a gun is pulled. In these environments, where so many people are packed in condensed areas, it can be easy to inflict maximum damage with minimal effort. Pulling a trigger as a means of deescalating a hostile situation has become far more common.

A Look To The Future:

There are a multitude of ways to go about limiting mass shootings. These solutions start with going after politicians who sit idly by as shootings persist, refusing to help put restrictions on gun purchasing and possession. Democrats have been largely unsuccessful at convincing members across the aisle to join the cause, but that can change. In the end, it is a numbers game and trying to persuade the entire GOP is not feasible. However, targeting moderate Republicans, especially those in opposition to the MAGA movement, may help provide the numbers for a potential bill to pass in both the Senate and the House. There has already begun to be a ripple in the Republican Party over the U.S’s current relationship with Israel, leading to increased bipartisan support in attempting to change the level of support for Benjamin Netanyahu’s far right government. Using this possible new alliance to also convince these moderates of the dangers that guns pose to the general public could be a viable solution.

A step down, targeting the NRA can lead to decreases in mass shooting frequency. It would likely violate the constitution to, without viable cause, prevent the amount of funding they can accumulate for Republicans. But, if their organization can be linked to unethical gun sales and careless procedures that enable guns to fall into the wrong hands, perhaps they must make internal changes before they are allowed to contribute to campaigns. Either they fail to provide support for Republicans, thereby breaking the strength of the need for Republicans to blindly oppose gun restriction, or the NRA actually improves its safety regulations. This yields an excellent opportunity for a win-win.

If the push for legislative change proves unsuccessful, other methods are still available. Prevention is possible through human connection and proactive intervention, especially in the case of school shootings. By prioritizing anonymous reporting for "leaked" plans and replacing zero-tolerance punishment with care teams that support students in crisis, communities can disrupt the isolation and despair that fuel these tragedies. Students, especially those in areas where resources dedicated to mental facilities are lacking, would greatly benefit from programs that promote inclusivity. Perhaps starting at a younger age may help them never feel the need to venture to the dark web for individuals in similar positions to them. Children feeling like they belong could very well prevent the need for violence down the line since the sheer anger that bubbles and becomes reinforced from social media algorithms ceases to exist.

The Harsh Reality:

Alas, we will be left wanting more change than we will see. Despite the best efforts of organizations and individuals to move toward a future where people can exist without fear of being struck down, there are too many obstacles. A grassroots effort will not change the fundamental way that the NRA lobbies, and Republicans will continue to have their wallets filled. There will be no reason for them to venture out and support anti-gun legislation and so the new bills that reach the Senate and House floor will not receive the needed votes and support. Even if moderate Republicans were successfully recruited, they would likely ask for concessions that the Democrats would be unwilling to agree on, and a deadlock would form.

More fundamentally concerning is the truth that humans are headed for more isolating times, not less. Companies value our engagement more than our free will, and if they can keep people entranced by their phones, their profits will continue to soar. More isolation means more feelings of not belonging and more desperate attempts to fit in. This welcomes in even more communities united by twisted logic with heightened capabilities of provoking violence. Add in the ease of access to assault-grade weapons, and it feels quite helpless. 130 years of American history has perfectly sculpted an environment that serves as a breeding ground for mass shootings. We are built for bloodshed, and unnecessary death will follow us into the coming decades.
